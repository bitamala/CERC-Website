The Challenge

The Real Cost of Corruption

Corruption is one of the most corrosive issues of our time. It wastes public resources, widens economic and social inequalities, breeds discontent and political polarisation and reduces trust in institutions.

Corruption perpetuates inequality and poverty, impacting well-being and undermining opportunities to participate equally in social, economic and political life.

Our Response

Integrity as a Strategy

A strategic and sustainable response to corruption is integrity building. Integrity is essential for building strong institutions and assures citizens that the government is working in their interest.

Integrity is not just a moral issue — it is about making economies more productive, public sectors more efficient, and societies more inclusive. It is about restoring trust.

About CERC

Who We Are

The Centre de Recherche sur l'Anti-Corruption — a DRC-registered NGO committed to transparency, accountability, and ethical conduct across the Congo.

CERC is a DRC-registered organization committed to promoting transparency, accountability, and ethical conduct within communities. Through various initiatives, CERC seeks to empower individuals to demand integrity in public services, fostering a culture of accountability and combating corruption at the grassroots level. It has emerged as a pioneer in introducing creative approaches to promote social accountability and anti-corruption efforts in education and health sectors in South-Kivu, North-Kivu and Kinshasa.

Our Mission

  • Advance knowledge on the causes and consequences of corruption and support the development of new anti-corruption policies and initiatives in the DRC.
  • Empower young people with skills and tools to create positive change towards more accountable, open and inclusive institutions and public services.

Our Vision

CERC is working towards a Congo in which:

  • Government, public institutions, politics, businesses and civil society are free from corruption.
  • Young people are empowered with knowledge and skills to take action to claim and defend their rights.
  • Barriers to meaningful participation are removed and equitable partnerships are encouraged.
  • Youth leadership capacity emerges to hold governments accountable to the needs and aspirations of citizens.

Our History

How We Started

Founded in April 2017 in the Democratic Republic of the Congo with a clear mission: use integrity as a sustainable response to corruption.

"How can integrity be a sustainable response to conflict and instability in eastern Congo?"

CERC was founded in April 2017 to find answers to three fundamental questions that lie at the heart of our work:

How can communities obtain better services using integrity?

How can we teach integrity to equip the next generation of leaders to say no to corruption?

How can integrity be a sustainable response to conflict and instability in eastern Congo?

We began to address these challenging questions by focusing our attention on research and investment in education and training for young people, helping them develop the skills to hold their leaders accountable, cultivating a new generation of ethical leaders, and combating inefficient and corrupt public services and infrastructure projects in their communities.

By 2022, we had reached 3,060 young people through our investment in education and training. They are now joining forces to monitor vital projects and services in their communities and working with their leaders to solve problems identified through community monitoring. Their work has benefited over a million lives in our coverage areas.

Transparency

Good Governance

Our governing structure is transparent and conforms to relevant laws. We seek to follow the principles of best practices in governance.

Governing Structure

  • A governing body which supervises and evaluates the executive director and oversees program and budgetary matters. It defines overall strategy, ensures resources are used efficiently, performance is measured, and financial integrity is assured.
  • Written procedures determining the appointment, responsibilities, and terms of members of the governing body, preventing and managing conflicts of interest.
  • A regular general meeting with the authority to appoint and replace members of the governing body.

Professional Management

  • Financial Controls: Internal financial control procedures ensure all funds are used effectively and minimize the risk of misuse, following best practices in financial management.
  • Evaluation: Defined evaluation procedures for boards, staff, programs, and projects based on mutual accountability, seeking to improve effectiveness on an ongoing basis.
  • Partners: Organizational integrity includes ensuring partners meet the highest standards of honesty and accountability, with no dealings with organizations involved in illegal or unethical practices.
  • Human Resources: HR policies conform fully with international and national labor regulations, applying best voluntary sector practices for employee rights, health and work/life balance.
  • Anti-Bribery: HR policies specifically prohibit acts of bribery or corruption by staff or any persons working for, or on behalf of, the organization.
  • Whistleblowers: Staff members are encouraged to draw management's attention to activities that may not comply with the law or our mission and commitments.

Accountability

Whistleblowing Policy

CERC is seriously committed to maintaining practices of internal accountability and providing safe, confidential channels to report wrongdoing.

The Centre de Recherche sur l'Anti-Corruption requires all its employees to abide by all laws, rules and regulations applicable to it — both in the Democratic Republic of the Congo and in each province to which its operations extend — and to conduct its activities in accordance with the highest ethical standards of integrity, as set forth in its Code of Conduct. This policy outlines a procedure to enable all individuals associated with CERC to voice concerns in a responsible and effective manner.

Who Can Report

  • Staff members and consultants
  • Board members and grantees
  • Partners and volunteers
  • Members of the public

What to Report

  • Violations of applicable laws or ethical obligations
  • Fraudulent or dishonest use or misuse of resources or property
  • Activities that may not comply with the law or CERC's mission

How to Report

  • Report to the Executive Director through standard channels
  • Report directly to the Board of Directors if uncomfortable with standard channels
  • Reports accepted in writing, by email, or orally
  • Anonymous reports are accepted and will be investigated

Protection Against Retaliation

  • CERC staff, consultants, board members, grantees, partners, and volunteers may not retaliate against a whistleblower for reporting activity believed to be illegal, fraudulent, dishonest or unethical.
  • Prohibited retaliation includes threats of physical harm, loss of job, punitive work assignments, or impact on salary or fees.
  • Whistleblowers who believe they have been retaliated against may file a written complaint with the President of the Board. All complaints will be promptly investigated.
  • All information provided will be treated in full confidence by CERC, except where legal obligation requires disclosure to public authorities.

Approach

Theory of Change

A holistic approach to promoting integrity through knowledge & capacity-building, movement building, and influencing decision-makers.

Development works best when young people participate in community problem solving — when young people work with their leaders to ensure their needs are met and public commitments are fulfilled.

CERC partners with young people to capture critical insights on governance and accountability issues and provide them with knowledge, skills, and tools to design and implement solutions.

How It Works

Give Young People a Stake in Their Communities

Our programs help young people engage in the planning and monitoring of government programs in their communities. Young people gain and share knowledge to help communities make decisions, along with the skills to constructively engage with authorities and hold public officials accountable. Increased youth participation means more public oversight and less opportunity for corruption.

Community Feedback Mechanisms

For effective accountability, young people need standards, indicators, and monitoring frameworks. Our programs use community monitoring on the quality of services using citizen reports, community scorecards, citizen budget monitoring, expenditure tracking, and media pressure to produce data that assesses performance.

Connecting Youth with Local Governments

Our programs encourage dialogue between young people and their governments. Many focus on improving citizen feedback to service providers, supported by hard evidence and data and preparation of authorities to receive citizen feedback. The result is better communication, better decision-making, and better development outcomes.

Initiative

Integrity Clubs in Schools

Student-led spaces for young people to learn and practice integrity — monitoring education services and advocating for better schools through the EduCheck mobile application.

The Problem We Are Addressing

Corruption in education has a significant impact on the ability of young people to access future opportunities. Having to pay for school when it should be free, teacher absenteeism and lack of resources are examples of how corruption prevents access to education. Even though the budget allocated to education in the DRC increased from 6.5% to 20% by 2020, students, parents, and communities lack the information and capacity to hold schools accountable.

Without reliable data, the learning crisis remains invisible, goals cannot be set, and policy efforts cannot be monitored for impact. CERC addresses these challenges by providing the Ministry of Education and school authorities with reliable, real-time data at the school level.

Our Solution: EduCheck

Our unique approach focuses on frontline data collected by students through our mobile application "EduCheck". Reliable and readily available data lie at the heart of efforts to ensure quality education and safe schools.

For Education Officials

Our solution helps produce reliable, real-time data that can be used by the Ministry of Education to shape policy and allocate resources to schools. Problems with policy implementation can be corrected in real time, failures adjusted, and outcomes measured.

For Students

CERC engages students to improve their learning conditions through self-advocacy to ensure quality education and safe educational facilities, creating Integrity Clubs — student-led spaces to learn and practice integrity.

Integrity Leadership Course

Holistic, innovative training helping organizations build institutional integrity and eliminate systemic corruption — inspired by the four pillars of integrity.

CERC provides training and mentoring on building institutional integrity and anti-corruption, helping local governments, ministries, agencies, public enterprises, private businesses, civil society organizations and others eliminate, minimize and prevent systemic corruption.

We understand integrity to be the alignment of four factors: Accountability, Competence, Ethics and Corruption Control — helping clients restore health to their organizations.

⚖️

Accountability

The ability of stakeholders to check that we do what we say we do.

🎯

Competence

The ability to deliver quality and perform well.

🌿

Ethics

Behaving impartially and reliably with a public purpose.

🔒

Corruption Control

Preventing the abuse of power or position for personal gain.

Activities Supported

  • Designing and conducting integrity assessments to identify systemic vulnerabilities to corruption
  • Designing and facilitating the development of long-term anti-corruption strategic and action plans
  • Designing and implementing capacity development interventions: workshops, training, seminars, online courses, campaigns
  • Facilitating internal change and reform processes at all levels of organizations and government
  • Designing and conducting integrity building leadership courses including peer-to-peer exchange and coaching
  • Elaborating effective solutions to identified systemic institutional weaknesses to increase transparency and accountability
  • Conducting transparency and accountability analyses
  • Developing Codes of Ethics in a participatory manner and creating conditions for their effective implementation
  • Developing Integrity Pacts between public institutions and businesses
  • Developing community relations to create collaborative ways to deal with corruption

Research & Learning in the Education Sector (2021)

CERC conducted in-depth assessments on corruption and accountability in the DRC education sector with 54 senior government education officials and 19 accountability practitioners and anti-corruption experts. The work included 4 consultative workshops, the Integrity Knowledge, Attitude, and Practice Survey in 41 secondary schools in South Kivu and Kinshasa, and a compilation of existing studies. Findings were used to influence policy changes and stimulate debate on corruption in the DRC education sector.

Civil Society Parallel Report on UNCAC Implementation (2021)

As an active member of the UNCAC Coalition, in June 2021 CERC was commissioned to conduct the CSO Parallel assessment of UNCAC implementation by the DRC, covering Chapter II (Preventive Measures) and Chapter V (Asset Recovery). CERC met with state and non-state actors between June 11 and December 1, 2021 to collect data. The research produced a parallel civil society report highlighting the DRC's efforts, evidence of good practices, and gaps in implementing anti-corruption and asset recovery measures.

Gender Equality & Social Inclusion

Empowering women, girls, and all individuals at risk of exclusion to act with and demand integrity in the DRC.

Equal access to quality education is vital for the future of any country and is the key to sustainable development. Without the equal participation of all people, including individuals at risk of exclusion, society has less opportunity to reach its full potential in terms of its economy and level of governance.

Around the world, girls suffer from injustice, difficulty accessing school, violence, and child marriage and labor. Missing education means girls and women will remain underrepresented in future leadership. Quality education can give girls and boys the knowledge and skills to behave responsibly and actively participate in social, economic, and political decision-making.

At CERC, our goal is to empower women, girls, and all individuals at risk of exclusion within the communities in which we work to act with and demand integrity, actively taking part in building institutions to promote a Congo that is open, accountable, and responsive to their needs and expectations.

Since 2017, through the NORAD-funded project "Students Acting for Honesty, Integrity and Equality," CERC campaigns for the achievement of SDG 4 (quality education) and SDG 5 (gender equality and empowerment of all women and girls).

Initiative

Promoting Rule of Law

Strengthening judicial independence and equal access to justice for all Congolese citizens across the DRC.

In the Democratic Republic of the Congo, the justice sector lacks the capacity and independence to advance its own reform and provide quality, fair, and equitable services to all Congolese. The judiciary has a limited reach in the country and is deprived of the resources needed to fulfill its mandate, undermining the state's role in ensuring equal access to justice and exacerbating petty corruption within the system.

Political interference puts at risk the independence of the judiciary and citizens' right to a fair trial. Across the country, poverty, lack of judicial resources, and widespread corruption prevent Congolese from seeking justice, further marginalizing youth and women.

IF judicial institutions increase internal transparency, accountability, and integrity; and civil society is empowered to monitor the justice system — THEN judicial independence and public trust in the judiciary will increase, allowing the judiciary to deliver justice to the people of the DRC effectively.

Goal

The goal of this initiative is to strengthen the institutional independence of the judiciary and individual judges to advance the justice system's efforts to fight corruption and promote equal and equitable access to justice in the DRC, ultimately improving public perception and trust of the judiciary.

Objectives

Transparency & Accountability

Increased transparency, accountability, and integrity in the justice system to promote equal and equitable justice and support the DRC's anti-corruption efforts.

Civil Society Empowerment

Empowered civil society to demand government commitment to judicial independence and contribute meaningfully to judicial reform processes.

Equal Access to Justice

Increased equal and equitable access to justice for all Congolese citizens, regardless of wealth, gender or social status.

Initiative

UN Convention against Transnational Organized Crime

Research and advocacy addressing how corruption facilitates human trafficking, migrant smuggling, and organized crime in the DRC.

In the Democratic Republic of the Congo, it is estimated that there are more than one million victims of human trafficking, most involving forced labour in artisanal mining sites, agriculture, domestic servitude, recruitment of children by armed groups, and the trafficking of migrants for sexual purposes.

CERC launched research to determine how corruption facilitates migrant smuggling and human trafficking in the DRC. The research findings are used to develop policies and programs to combat organized crime and stimulate debate around the fight against human trafficking and migration in the Congo.

Our Work Includes

  • Lobbying with the Ministry of Justice to pass legislation criminalizing all forms of trafficking with sufficiently stringent penalties, including sex trafficking commensurate with other serious crimes
  • Developing programs that increase efforts to address all forms of trafficking, including sex trafficking and labour trafficking of both adults and children
  • Implementing investigation programs to proactively identify trafficking victims, including training front-line officials to identify vulnerable groups, and refer victims to appropriate care
  • Sensitizing armed groups to cease unlawful use of children in armed conflicts and artisanal mining
  • Providing comprehensive protection services to victims of all forms of trafficking
  • Developing and implementing procedures for collecting and reporting data on sex trafficking and forced labour
  • Using existing legislation to increase efforts to investigate, prosecute, convict, and adequately sentence traffickers, including complicit officials
  • Raising public awareness about human trafficking

UNTOC Review Mechanism

The United Nations Convention against Transnational Organized Crime (UNTOC), adopted by General Assembly resolution 55/25 on November 15, 2000, is the main international instrument in the fight against transnational organized crime. The DRC ratified the UNTOC on October 25, 2005.

The Convention represents a major step forward in the fight against transnational organized crime. Two CERC officers participated in a UNODC-organized Workshop on the UNTOC Self-Assessment Questionnaire and the Self-paced Course on the UNTOC Review Mechanism, reinforcing the DRC's commitment to measures against money laundering, corruption, obstruction of justice, and the three supplementary Protocols on human trafficking, migrant smuggling, and firearms trafficking.
